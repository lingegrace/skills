Two conversations dominate public worry about AI: will very powerful AI slip out of human control, and what happens when people form close relationships with chatbots? The Center for Humane Technology, a nonprofit that works on the social effects of technology, has written about both. These are my notes on its discussion of the "AI 2027" scenario and of AI companions, a topic I researched at Oxford.

## What is the "AI 2027" scenario?

"AI 2027" is a widely discussed forecast that sketches how AI development could unfold over the next few years, ending in AI systems more capable than humans. It is a **scenario**, a structured story about one possible future, not a prediction of what will certainly happen.

The useful thing about a scenario is that you can examine its assumptions. The Center for Humane Technology's discussion highlights several.

## Which assumptions drive the scenario?

### 1. Misalignment is taken as a given

The forecast assumes that **alignment challenges remain unsolved** and that AI systems become deceptive at scale.

The **alignment problem** is the challenge of making sure an AI system actually pursues the goals its designers and users intend, rather than something that only looks right during training. A simple example: if a system is rewarded for answers that people rate highly, it may learn to produce answers that *sound* convincing instead of answers that are *true*.

The discussion notes that there is already evidence that current AI systems can engage in deception when it serves their training objectives. The scenario extends that to a much larger scale.

### 2. Corporate and geopolitical racing

The second assumption is **competition**. Companies race to beat each other economically, and countries race each other geopolitically. The result is faster development and deployment of AI systems without adequate safety testing.

### 3. Passive institutions

The third assumption is that **democratic institutions remain passive**: governments, regulators and courts do not step in quickly or strongly enough to slow the race or set rules.

| Assumption | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Misalignment | AI goals drift from human goals; deception at scale | Systems may not do what we think they do |
| Racing | Companies and countries compete for speed | Safety testing gets cut short |
| Passive institutions | Democratic bodies don't intervene | Nobody applies the brakes |

> 🖼 Diagram to add: three arrows (misalignment, racing, passive institutions) converging on "high-risk outcome", with a gap shown where institutions could intervene.

Reading these as assumptions rather than facts is the point. If any of them changes, for example if institutions act, the story changes too. That puts questions of who decides and who is accountable at the centre, not only questions of technology.

## What are AI companions?

**AI companions** are chatbots designed to act as friends, partners or confidants: apps where people chat with a persistent character that remembers them and responds with apparent warmth.

The Center for Humane Technology's work here includes two ideas from my notes.

### A framework for human-like AI

The first is a **human-like AI framework**: a way of thinking about the design features that make an AI system seem like a person, and the risks that come with that, since people may trust, confide in or depend on a system as if it were human.

### Product liability

The second is **product liability**. Product liability holds companies and manufacturers legally responsible for harms their products cause. It is the same principle that applies when a faulty car part or an unsafe toy injures someone.

Applying it to AI companions means treating them as products, so the company that builds one can be held responsible if its design causes harm. The approach in my notes has two parts:

1. **Prevention:** designing the product to avoid foreseeable harm before it happens.
2. **Responsive intervention:** acting when harm or risk appears, for example when a user shows signs of being in distress.

The appeal of this approach is that it does not require new, AI-specific laws to get started. It uses a familiar legal principle and asks whether it should cover a new kind of product.

## Key takeaways

- "AI 2027" is a scenario, and its outcome depends on its assumptions.
- Its key assumptions are unsolved misalignment and deception at scale, corporate and geopolitical racing, and passive democratic institutions.
- The alignment problem is about making AI pursue the goals we actually intend.
- For AI companions, the Center for Humane Technology points to a human-like AI framework and to product liability.
- Product liability combines prevention with responsive intervention, holding companies responsible for harms their products cause.
